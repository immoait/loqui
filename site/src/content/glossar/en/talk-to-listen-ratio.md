## How to calculate it

Your talk ratio is your speaking time divided by the total speaking time on the call. Silence doesn’t count. If 30 minutes of the call are spoken and you talk for 18 of them, your talk ratio is 60%. Written as a talk-to-listen ratio, that’s 60:40.

It’s hard to gauge on your own. Most people underestimate how much they talk, especially when they know their product well.

## Benchmarks

The number you’ll see quoted most is **43:57**: the rep talks 43% of the time, the buyer 57%. It comes from [Gong’s analysis of 25,537 B2B sales calls](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/taking-the-guesswork-out-of-sales-call-effectiveness/), recorded in the first half of 2016 and published in 2017.

[Gong published a new analysis in 2025](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio/) of 326,000 sales calls of at least 10 minutes. The average rep still talked 60% of the time. Won deals averaged 57% rep talk time, lost deals 62%.

Treat these as reference points, not targets. They average across many kinds of calls, and the right ratio depends on where you are in the deal.

## Why it matters in sales

Discovery is about understanding the buyer’s problem. Every minute you talk is a minute the buyer isn’t telling you about it. A high talk ratio at this stage often leads to proposals that miss what the buyer actually needs.

Later stages are different. On a demo or when you walk through pricing, you’ll naturally talk more.

Loqui measures your talk ratio live, keeping your microphone separate from the meeting app’s audio. In the bar at the top of your screen, a small meter shows the last two minutes. If your share goes above 70%, or you talk for more than about 45 seconds straight, Loqui gives you a quick nudge. Your post-call feedback shows your talk ratio for the whole call.
