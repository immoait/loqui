Most reps don’t talk too much because they’re bad at selling. They do it because silence feels risky and they know their product cold. The fix isn’t to “be quieter.” It’s a handful of habits that hand the call back to the buyer. Gong’s analysis of 25,537 B2B sales calls (recorded in 2016, published in 2017) put the ideal talk-to-listen ratio at 43:57: you talk 43% of the time, the buyer talks 57%. Its 2025 data from 326,000 calls found reps still average 60%, and won deals averaged 57%. Here’s how to get closer to the buyer doing most of the talking on your next discovery call.

## 1. Ask open questions

Questions that start with “how,” “what,” or “walk me through” can’t be answered with a yes or no. “Walk me through how your team handles this today” gets you far more than “Do you use spreadsheets for that?”

## 2. Wait three seconds after every answer

Buyers often say the most important thing after a short pause, once they notice you’re still listening. The silence feels longer to you than it does to them. Count to three before you respond.

## 3. Summarize instead of explaining

> “If I’m hearing you right, this is less about cost and more about the time your team is losing. Is that fair?”

It shows you were listening, and it invites the buyer to add to it or correct you. Either way, they’re the one talking.

## 4. Hold the demo until later

If you’re sharing your screen five minutes in, you’re answering questions nobody has asked yet. Try this instead: “Happy to show you that in a minute. First, I want to make sure I understand…”

## 5. Track your own talk time

It’s hard to change what you don’t measure. Loqui shows your talk ratio live during the call and nudges you when you’ve been talking too long. Your post-call feedback shows your talk ratio for the whole call.
